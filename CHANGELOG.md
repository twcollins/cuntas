# Changelog

## Unreleased

USA phase 4: Social Security, Roth notes and US people with money outside the US. The US 2026 pack gains a Social Security section (SSA's 2026 bend points of $1,286 and $7,749, the 90/32/15% formula, 35 computation years, 40 credits, full retirement age by year of birth, early-claiming reductions and delayed credits, written as the fractions SSA states) and a foreign-reporting section (FBAR's $10,000, Form 8938's thresholds for people living in the US and abroad, the June 15 filing date for citizens abroad and the $132,900 foreign earned income exclusion), all provisional with sources. Retirement now counts US Social Security beside the Irish and UK State Pensions for anyone who lives in the US, is a US citizen, or has US work recorded: from the benefit on the person's SSA statement if they enter it, otherwise estimated from today's pay for each year of US work (assumed since 22 for a US resident until they say), and a new card shows the monthly benefit for each claiming age from 62 to 70. Someone living outside the US is asked for their SSA statement figure, since their US earnings aren't known here. US residents now default to retiring at full retirement age (67) rather than the Irish State Pension age, and Retirement hides the Irish and UK contribution years from people with no link to either. When a US member's income is in or above the Roth IRA phase-out, the IRA step explains the backdoor Roth and the pro-rata rule (Form 8606); when someone plans to retire before claiming Social Security and has 401(k) or IRA money, Retirement notes that those low-income years suit Roth conversions. The Tax centre has a new card for US citizens anywhere and US residents with accounts outside the US: each account's highest and latest balance in the year in dollars, whether the FBAR and Form 8938 are needed and when they're due, the filing date and exclusion for citizens living abroad, and notes on PFICs (brokerage and Investment Account holdings), ISAs (not tax-free for US tax), foreign pensions and joint accounts, each with its source; this year so far or last year. On the Irish ladder, the "save or invest" card tells a US citizen living in Ireland or the UK that funds there are PFICs. The schema adds `usCoveredYears`, `usSsStatementMinor` and `usClaimAge` on members (null for existing members); no migration is needed. Tests: golden tests for the PIA formula (SSA's own example), full retirement age, every claiming age from 62 to 70, the estimate from pay, and FBAR and Form 8938 thresholds; engine tests for the backdoor Roth note and the PFIC note; ledger tests for highest balances, the reporting card's figures and notes, and Social Security in the projection; end-to-end tests for a US citizen in the Irish demo household and a new US household.

Mobile apps phases 1 to 3: native storage, the rest of the phone layout, and files in and out. Everything that touches the device now goes through a small platform layer (`apps/web/src/lib/platform/`): on the web it is IndexedDB and downloads as before; in the iOS and Android apps household data is kept in an encrypted SQLite database (SQLCipher, with the key in the Keychain or behind an Android Keystore key) instead of the web view's storage, which the OS can clear. Each save is one write, applied in one transaction in the apps, so the household and the household list stay in step. Anyone who ran an earlier app build gets their data copied across on first launch: copy, read back, compare, then delete the old copy; if anything doesn't match, the old copy is left as it was, the app keeps using it, and Settings says so with a prompt to export a backup. Saving also happens when the app goes to the background, since the OS can end it without warning. In the apps the status bar follows the light or dark theme, the splash screen stays until the data has loaded, and the web view shrinks above the keyboard with the focused field kept in view. Exports (JSON, all CSV files at once, the encrypted backup, rule-file drafts) open the share sheet in the apps (Save to Files, AirDrop, Drive, email). Statements, rule files and backups can be opened in Cúntas from elsewhere: dropped on any page, opened from Files or another app on a phone, or with "Open with" for the installed web app on a desktop. Each goes to the page that reads it (statements to Import, rule files to Tax rules, backups and JSON exports to Settings, which asks before replacing the household). Overview asks "Back up this household" once the last backup or JSON export is 30 days old and the household has changed since; "Not now" hides it for 30 days, and Settings shows when the last one was made. On Android the database is kept out of cloud backup and device transfer, since its key can't leave the phone. No schema change and no migration (the backup dates are kept in the device's household list). Not yet tried on a device: no Xcode or Android SDK on the build machine, so the native code is type-checked and `cap sync`ed but not compiled; the device checklist in `docs/design/mobile-apps.md` is still to run. Tests: the store against an in-memory platform (a change survives a relaunch; removing and wiping delete the records), the copy from IndexedDB (moved, nothing to move, a mismatch or a failed write leaves the original), the backup reminder's timing, and routing opened files by kind; end-to-end tests for a rule file and an export dropped on another page, the backup record and the reminder a month on.

UX phase 6: a setup checklist, a phone layout and empty states that say what to do. A new household's Overview shows "Get set up" in place of step 0 until four lines are done: You (date of birth, work, pay and, for a US resident, state, asked in a sheet on the page; onboarding still asks only for a name and country), Accounts (import statements or add an account in a sheet), Spending (import, add a spend, or give a rough monthly figure) and Budget (one tap makes a first envelope budget from last period's spending, else the latest month with spending, else the estimate). Each line counts once ever, so a new month doesn't bring the checklist back; it can also be hidden. From the checklist, a new US user reaches a first budget in 13 actions: onboarding, details, two statements, the budget. On phones, a bottom tab bar (Overview, Transactions, + Add in the centre, Next steps, More for the full menu) replaces the menu button and the floating + button, the page respects the safe areas of notched screens, and Transactions is a list grouped by date where tapping a row opens it to change the category or delete a hand-entered entry. Empty Transactions, Accounts, Debts, Goals, Pensions, Retirement and What-if pages now offer the action they need. Fixed: Budgets and Compare wrappers scrolled sideways on phones (grid columns now shrink to the screen), a chart tooltip near the right edge could widen the page, and a change made just before closing or reloading the page could be lost (it is now written when the page is hidden; a reload within about a third of a second of a change can still lose it). The schema adds `household.setup` (empty for existing households); no migration is needed. Tests: ledger tests for the checklist, the You details and the starter budget; end-to-end tests for the checklist path, the estimate path, and the phone tab bar with no page scrolling sideways at 375 px.

UX phase 5: categorising by merchant. Imported payees are cleaned into names a person recognises ("TRADER JOE'S #123 SAN FRANCISCO CA" becomes "Trader Joe's"): card-terminal wording, processor prefixes such as "SQ *", "TST*" and "PAYPAL *", store, card and phone numbers, ACH references and US town-and-state suffixes are removed, and a few big merchants with messy bank text (Amazon, Walmart, CVS) are named outright; the bank's text is kept for search and rules. The merchant dictionary adds about 400 US merchants and bill payees (grocers, utilities, carriers, insurers, fuel, transit, restaurants, shops, airlines, hotels, student and car loans), and card payments seen on the card ("Payment, thank you") are filed as transfers. Some Irish and UK patterns that misfired on US text now match whole words only: "fee" no longer matches "coffee", "Costa" no longer matches "Costa Mesa", "Haven" no longer matches "New Haven", "Iceland" no longer matches "Icelandair". On a typical month of US bank text, under 5% of spending is now left uncategorised, about 10% leaving rent aside (the target was under 20%). A new Review page shows the remaining uncategorised rows one merchant at a time, biggest total first: one tap on a category chip (or keys 1 to 4) files every row from that merchant and, unless unticked, adds a rule so the next import files it too; Undo stays for 10 seconds; Skip (S, or a swipe left on a phone) moves on. Review appears under Transactions in the sidebar with a count while anything is waiting, Transactions links to it, and Import's Review now opens it. Existing payees are not rewritten; the queue cleans them as it groups. No schema change and no migration. Tests: a table of real-looking bank strings for cleaning, dictionary cases for US merchants and the misfires, the under-a-fifth check on a US month, and the queue (grouping, ordering, filing, rules, undo); end-to-end tests for the queue on desktop and phone.

UX phase 4: import that finishes the job. The Import page takes several statement files at once, and files dropped on any page open Import with them. Each file gets an account without asking: the one whose earlier imports had the same account number or IBAN (Barclays, AIB, Capital One and Amex exports carry one, as do OFX and CAMT.053 files; a masked card number matches its full number), else the one that last took the same bank's layout, else an existing account of the same kind named after the bank, else a new account named after the bank, as a credit card for card exports, in the file's currency. The guess can be changed, but two statements need nothing more than pressing Import. A CSV in an unknown layout opens the column mapper with the guess applied, including the date format read from the rows, and a live preview; the mapping is kept for the next file with the same header row. After importing, a result screen says how many transactions came in, how many were already there, matched to hand-entered rows or paired as transfers, how many were categorised automatically and how many need a category (with Review now, which opens Transactions on the uncategorised rows), and the spending over the dates covered, with a link to set up a budget. Step 0 now counts any transaction in the last 60 days as tracking, so last month's statement imported late in this month counts, and Overview's spending card shows the latest month with spending, with its dates, when this period has none yet. The schema adds optional `importHints` on accounts and `importMappings` on the dataset (empty for existing households); no migration is needed. The snapshot's `transactionsLast30Days` and `uncategorisedLast30Days` are now `...Last60Days`. Tests: importer tests for account numbers, card types, header keys and the date-format guess; ledger tests for account matching, planning and importing several files, the summary, remembered mappings, the 60-day rule and the spending fallback; end-to-end tests for two files at once, a drop on Overview, a remembered mapping and the phone layout.

UX phase 3: a rough monthly spending figure. People who haven't imported, or not enough, can say roughly what they spend in a month on seven lines (rent or mortgage, groceries, utilities and phone, transport, childcare, debt repayments, everything else), prefilled from the household's rent, mortgage and debt payments. Until transactions cover two months, the estimate stands in for them: its essential lines are the household's essential monthly spending and feed the payday waterfall. When nobody's pay is recorded, the emergency-fund targets on both ladders use essential spending instead of falling back to the floor, and the card says so, so a new household with no transactions gets a real target. Overview says when the target rests on the estimate, with a link to change it, and asks for a figure when there's nothing to go on. On Budgets, one tap turns the estimate into a first envelope budget, or opens the form and makes the budget from it. Budgets' waterfall now uses the starter-fund target for the emergency-fund bucket rather than one month's pay. The schema adds `household.spendingEstimate` (null for existing households); no migration is needed. Tests: engine tests for the fallback on both ladders, ledger tests for storing, prefilling, the two-month rule, the snapshot and the budget, and an end-to-end path (onboarding, estimate, three quick adds, budget) on desktop and phone.

UX phase 2: quick add. A **+ Add** button on every page (in the sidebar on desktop, a floating button on phones, and `N` on a keyboard) records a spend, money received or a move between your own accounts in a few taps: amount, a category chip, Save. Typing a payee fills the category from your past entries and the merchant dictionary; "This happens every month" also adds the payment to Bills; "Save and add another" keeps the sheet open. Spending with no account chosen goes to a Cash account created on first use, whose balance isn't tracked, so cash spending counts towards budgets and the plan without pulling net worth below zero. Rows entered by hand are marked "by hand" on Transactions and can be deleted there. When a bank import, file or sync, contains a payment you already entered on that account (same amount, within 3 days), it is merged into your entry, keeping your payee, category and note, instead of being counted twice. Fixed: "Netflix" was categorised as Commute and fuel because the dictionary's "tfl" matched inside it; short brand names in that pattern now match whole words only. The schema adds optional `source` on transactions and `tracksBalance` on accounts; no migration is needed. Tests: ledger tests for manual entry, moves, bills, deletion, merging and suggestions; an end-to-end quick-add path on desktop and phone.

UX phase 1: a new household starts in the right country. Onboarding asks for your first name and adds you as a resident of the country you chose, so a US household's first Overview uses US rules and wording instead of Irish ones. The sidebar keeps the everyday pages at the top, with Bills, Debts and Goals under Next steps, and moves the rest under a collapsible More. US phase 3, batch 2: every state now has a pack. The remaining 26 (AL, AR, CA, CT, DE, DC, HI, KS, ME, MD, MN, MO, MT, NE, NJ, NM, NY, ND, OH, OK, OR, RI, SC, VT, WV, WI) bring all 50 states and D.C. in, each sourced to the state's revenue department, statute or official forms, all provisional. The state calculator gained the rules these need: brackets with the statute's own bases, standard deductions that phase out (Maine, South Carolina, Rhode Island in steps), step down (Alabama) or slide (Wisconsin), exemptions by AGI band (Maryland, Ohio, Alabama dependents) or reduced dollar for dollar (Connecticut), exemption credits that phase out (California, Maine) or stop at a limit (Oregon), deductions for federal income tax (Oregon capped, Missouri by share, Alabama in full), New York's supplemental tax that recaptures the lower brackets, Connecticut's add-back tables and personal tax credit, Missouri's per-spouse split of joint income, Ohio's zero band and joint filing credit, two-earner subtractions and credits (Maryland, Wisconsin, South Carolina), California's renter's credit, Minnesota's refundable child credit (state tax can go below zero), and Oregon's 0.1% statewide transit tax. Golden tests reproduce a published figure where the state gives one that maps onto a return (California's rate schedule and tax table, Oregon's example 3, Hawaii's and New Mexico's tables, New Jersey's Evans example, Connecticut's Table B, Minnesota's 2026 table, Missouri's withholding and estimate chart, Arkansas's and Alabama's withholding examples, Maryland, Delaware, D.C. and Maine) and are worked by hand from the published rules otherwise. Where 2026 figures aren't out (California's indexing, Vermont's deductions, D.C.'s standard deduction and others) the pack uses the latest official year and says so. The in-app Guide now covers US households: state, filing status, HSA and Indiana county on the Household page, USD accounts, the r/personalfinance order, federal plus state take-home, and US state packs on the Tax rules page.

Priority states made as complete as the rules allow: Indiana, Virginia and Texas. Virginia is new: its four brackets, standard deduction, $930 exemptions and $800 for 65+, the age deduction with its income phase-out, the filing-threshold cliff, the credit for low-income individuals (by family size against the poverty guideline) and the spouse tax adjustment for two-earner couples, with golden tests reproducing the 2026 withholding example, the rate schedule, the tax table and the Form 760 spouse-adjustment example. Indiana now carries all 92 county income tax rates (in effect January 1, 2026): a member picks their county and take-home includes it, an exception to leaving local taxes out because every Indiana resident pays one. Indiana also gains the renter's deduction (from the household's rent), the extra $500 exemption for low-income filers 65+, and a test reproducing the IT-40 booklet's worked example. Texas was already complete (no state or local income tax). State packs can now carry local rates, a renter's deduction, an age deduction, a filing threshold, a low-income credit and a spouse adjustment; Massachusetts gains its rent deduction.

US phase 3, batch 1: state income tax. Each US member can have a state; take-home, the what-if and the plan then include that state's income tax on the same return as federal (shared by income on a joint return). State packs are sub-packs like Scotland's (`rules/us/<state>/2026.yaml`, key `us/ca/2026`) with their own schema, so a state file loads through Tax rules, gets checked like the federal one, and is drafted for next year per state; the Tax rules screen lists them under US states, and the check prompts name the member's state. `usStateTax` covers the shapes states use: no wage tax, a flat rate or brackets on federal AGI, federal taxable income or gross wages, standard deductions (own or federal), exemptions per filer, by filing status, per dependent and for age 65+ with income limits, exemption credits, a child deduction that steps down with income (North Carolina), a Social Security and Medicare deduction (Massachusetts) and Utah's taxpayer credit. 24 states are in: the nine with no wage tax (AK, FL, NV, NH, SD, TN, TX, WA, WY) and AZ, CO, GA, ID, IL, IN, IA, KY, LA, MA, MI, MS, NC, PA, UT, each sourced to the state revenue department or statute and provisional. Golden tests reproduce published examples where they map onto a return (Kentucky's 2026 withholding formula, Illinois's IL-700-T, Georgia's 2026 employer's guide, Colorado's tax table) and are worked by hand from the published rules otherwise, saying so. Local income taxes are out at launch: a state's results say when its cities or counties levy one. A state without a pack yet leaves take-home federal only, and the household screen says so.

US phase 2, the rest: plan, limits, bank files and wording. A household where everyone lives in the US gets a US next-step ladder in the r/personalfinance order (budget, starter emergency fund, employer match, debt above 10%, 3-6 month fund, HSA, IRA, 401(k) to the limit, moderate debt and other goals), with thresholds in a provisional `rules/us/flowchart.yaml`; the engine now runs either ladder from one generic core. New calculators `us.retirementLimits` (401(k) with age-50 and age-60-63 catch-ups, IRA, Roth IRA and traditional IRA deduction phase-outs, HSA) and `us.estimatedTax` (Pub. 505 safe harbour and four due dates), with golden tests from the IRS worked examples in Pub. 590-A and Pub. 505; the 2026 pack gains their figures. Pub. 590-A's example 1 shows 6,825 where its own rounding rule gives 6,830; the test follows the rule and says why. Self-employed US members get a monthly estimated-tax set-aside. New pension kinds for traditional and Roth IRAs and HSAs, and an HSA-eligible plan setting per member. CSV presets for Chase (checking and card), Bank of America (checking and card), Wells Fargo, Capital One (card and 360), Citi, American Express and Discover; the CSV reader now finds a header below a summary block, flips issuers that export charges as positive, takes a sign from a type column, and keeps USD. US households see US wording and date style ("Checking account", "401(k) and IRAs", "Sep 3, 2026"), onboarding asks where you live, and the Tax centre adds US deadlines and a limits and estimated-tax card. The US bank layouts and flowchart thresholds come from secondary sources and are marked to check against real exports and the wiki.

US federal tax (US phase 2, tax rules). The US is now a tax jurisdiction: a member can be US resident, with a filing status (single, married filing jointly or separately, head of household; existing members load as single). The provisional 2026 federal pack (`rules/us/2026.yaml`) holds the brackets and standard deductions from Rev. Proc. 2025-32, the senior deduction, the child tax credit and its refundable part, Social Security (2026 wage base 184,500), Medicare and the additional Medicare tax, self-employment tax, the qualified business income deduction below its threshold, and 401(k), IRA and HSA limits, each with its IRS or SSA source. `us.payroll` works out federal income tax owed for the year, FICA or self-employment tax, and take-home; a joint return shares tax and credits between spouses by income, and children count on joint and head-of-household returns. Golden tests are worked by hand from the IRS rate schedules. A US workplace plan (new pension kind 401(k)) counts as a pre-tax deferral. The Tax centre's take-home calculator, the household's pay, the snapshot and the move what-if use it. US figures are yours to check: the Tax rules screen lists the pack as not checked, results say "Figures not checked (2026)", and from 1 November the app says 2027 figures are usually out (the IRS hadn't published them when this pack was made). State and local income tax, US payslip checks, US wrapper comparisons and US plan steps aren't included yet.

The "What if we moved" screen now includes the US. New treaty packs (`xb/ie-us`, `xb/uk-us`, provisional, references only) classify pay, pensions, State Pensions, ISAs and accounts for a US resident: private pensions and State Pensions are taxed only in the US, an ISA loses its exemption and its funds are flagged as PFICs, foreign accounts are flagged for FBAR reporting, and a kept Irish or UK job is taxed there with a US credit. Moving notes come from the treaty with every country a mover has pay, pensions or accounts in, each topic once. Take-home after a move to the US uses the federal pack below. US citizens living in Ireland or the UK are now shown as also taxed by the US (the treaties' saving clause), except on State Pensions. The demo dataset gains synthetic EUR/USD rates.

US support, phase 1 (groundwork). Countries and currencies are now open sets: USD joins EUR and GBP for accounts, goals, bills and a household's base currency. ECB rates are stored for every supported currency, and any pair converts through EUR, with the same arithmetic as before for EUR and GBP. Nationality can include the US. Tax rule packs gain a schema that every built-in pack passes, and rule files can be loaded on a device from Settings → Tax rules. A loaded file is checked for shape, a source link on every section, rates between 0 and 1, rising bands and a year that matches its dates, and errors name the line; it replaces the built-in pack for its year, and removing it brings the built-in back. The Tax rules screen downloads a draft for next year (last year's figures with the dates moved on, marked to check against each source), compares each figure with last year's, and records the date and SHA-256 fingerprint when you tick sections. Results that use unchecked or last year's figures say "Figures not checked", and banners on Overview and Tax prompt for checks. Packs maintained with the app (Ireland, UK, Scotland) need no check, so Irish and UK output is unchanged. Rule files and checks are included in the encrypted backup and removed by Delete everything. Migration `0003_currencies.sql` allows a USD base currency on the server.

Several households on one device: each household is stored separately, with a switcher at the top of the sidebar and a Households screen to add one (new, demo, or by invite link), switch, remove it from the device, delete it on the server, and manage who can open it, with invites that set the role. An Account & sync screen replaces the server card in Data & sync (now Settings): sign in or create a sign-in with a passkey, open server households on this device, and sync the open household; the server address comes from `VITE_API_URL`. Signing in no longer replaces local data with the first server household; every replacement asks first. Onboarding adds "I already use Cúntas", and an invite link works on a device with no household. A Guide page gives a short tour of every feature, and the light/dark toggle moves from the sidebar to Settings. On-device storage changes to one IndexedDB entry per household with no migration (not yet released), so existing local data is reset. Playwright covers two households on one device and the onboarding sign-in card.

AIB import now recognises the current-account CSV export, which has one Description column instead of Description1–3 and two-digit years. Its zero-amount follow-on rows (ATM location, card time, references) are folded into the transaction above, and so is the end-of-day balance they carry. When no bank preset matches a CSV, the column mapper now guesses columns from the header names instead of taking the first three columns in order.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-10-23

Completes the phase 3 and 4 features. Self-employed tax: Class S PRSI applied month by month across a rate change, and a Form 11 estimate with preliminary tax on the 90%, 100% and 105% bases, balance due, deadlines and a monthly set-aside, which the engine asks self-employed households to plan for. UK: student loan plans 1, 2, 4, 5 and postgraduate in take-home pay; the pension annual allowance with taper, carry forward and MPAA; and a Scottish income tax sub-pack. A payslip check for Irish payslips (emergency tax, credits or band not allocated, USC band, PRSI) and UK ones (tax code, NI, student loan). Recurring-bill detection with price rises plus an Irish and UK bill calendar; a retirement projection with Irish and UK State Pensions in real terms across three scenarios; an after-tax wrapper comparison; and a residence what-if driven by relocation notes in the cross-border pack. HSBC UK CSV import (two layouts). Light, dark and system themes. A server admin role (granted only from the database owner's shell) to create, rename, suspend and delete households and manage members, plus owner-level member management; migration `0002_admin.sql` adds `users.is_admin`, `households.suspended_at` and invite roles, guarded by a trigger so the API role cannot grant admin. Capacitor 8 iOS and Android projects, with API options for native origins and cross-site cookies. The dataset gains payslips, bills and new member fields, all defaulted so 0.1 exports still load.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-10-22

First build from `docs/SPEC.md` (phases 0–2, with parts of 3 and 4). Versioned IE and UK rule packs with golden tests; the next-step engine for the seven flowchart steps; bank-file importers with ten presets; a local-first React PWA covering ledger, budgets, debts, goals, pensions and a tax centre; and an optional self-hosted stack (Hono API, pg-boss worker, Postgres with row-level security) for passkey sign-in, household sharing, encrypted sync, ECB rates and PSD2 bank sync via Enable Banking.
